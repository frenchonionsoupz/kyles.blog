href="https://delacanvas.com/collections/prints/products/almost-there"><span>Dela Studios</span></a></strong></figcaption></figure></div><p><span>The chorus to Madhuvan by Goose plays over my car’s speaker:</span></p><p><span>“</span><em><span>Alllllways allllmost… there</span></em><span>.”</span></p><p><span>I recognize this feeling. Though maybe it’s the first time it’s been given words, I’ve lived my entire life with this feeling of being ‘always almost there.’ Always just around the corner from the version of myself I’m supposed to be.</span></p><p><span>It’s like my current self is always deficient by a few altered/added habits or routines I’ve reserved for some future version of me. In my mind, </span><em><span>then</span></em><span> I would be complete.</span></p><p><span>In his essay ‘Close’, David Whyte describes this feeling as being part of our hardwiring:</span></p><p style="text-align: center;"><em><span>“Our human essence lies not in arrival, but in being almost there; we are creatures who are on the way, our journey a series of impending anticipated arrivals.</span></em></p><p style="text-align: center;"><em><span>We live by unconsciously measuring the inverse distances of our proximity: an intimacy calibrated by the vulnerability we feel in giving up our sense of separation.”</span></em></p><p><span>So I wanted to explore </span><em><span>why</span></em><span> this would be such a hardwired condition. And the reason I’ve found most interesting is that:</span></p><p style="text-align: center;"><strong><span>It’s a way for us to resist change, disguised as forward motion</span></strong><span>.</span></p><p><span>This feeling of being ‘always almost there’ is a defense mechanism, used by the ego, to protect our Constructed Self from the unpredictability of the present moment.</span></p><h2><strong><span>How I think of this Constructed Self</span></strong></h2><p><span>To preface, I want to say that I never studied psychology, and I’m mostly just an idiot. These aren’t like </span><em><span>legit </span></em><span>textbook terms, just labels I’ve created to help me understand this phenomenon of ‘always almost there.’</span></p><p><span>But to understand why we stay ‘almost there,’ I think we start by considering how we build our identity. It seems to me that we navigate the world using this Constructed Self – the version of us that is legible to society:</span></p><p><span>“Kyle is a freelance copywriter…”<br>“Kyle has a dog named Goose…”<br>“Kyle is sort of a dork…”</span></p><p><span>This is especially important for us, given that we’re status-oriented creatures. And our evolution demands it – because for our ancestors, being rejected by the group could’ve meant being left alone to get slaughtered by some large animal.</span></p><p><span>Legibility equals safety:</span></p><p><span>– If the tribe can’t define you, they can’t trust you.<br>– If they can’t trust you, they cast you out.</span></p><p><span>So we build this Constructed Self. And it seems that this is built from two places:</span></p><p><span>1️⃣ </span><strong><span>External signals</span></strong></p><p><span>These are the materials we gather from the world to build our “case” for who we are.</span></p><p><span>→ </span><strong><span>Labels</span></strong><span>: Call someone a genius enough times – they’ll eventually believe it, buy Twitter, and think they understand the fate of the universe.</span></p><p><span>→ </span><strong><span>Feedback</span></strong><span>: When you sing, and everybody covers their ears with shrieks of horror, you may accrue the identity of “a bad singer.”</span></p><p><span>→ </span><strong><span>Roles</span></strong><span>: Parent, CEO, village drunk… the titles and roles we hold play a major role in how we see ourselves.</span></p><p><strong><span>2️⃣ An ideal self</span></strong></p><p><span>This is the internal part of the construction.</span></p><p><span>It’s the self that exists only in our minds or in some ideal future that we’re still working toward (‘always almost there’).</span></p><p><span>It’s built from our cultural inheritance, past experiences, and images we’ve borrowed from people we’re not.</span></p><h2><strong><span>Neither ‘Constructed Self’ Is Really </span></strong><em><strong><span>You</span></strong></em></h2><p><span>Maybe there’s no “real You” to point to at all – no </span><em><span>karmic ideal </span></em><span>that we are destined to become.</span></p><p><span>But if a true self did exist somewhere in the ether, it probably wouldn’t be a rigid blueprint we have to constantly manage and adjust. I guess it’d be a more fluid way of being than that.</span></p><p><span>We can live our lives in accordance with our desire to ‘fit in’ with others, in a way they approve of. Or we can live with the raw, immediate truth that comes from living fully in the present moment.</span></p><p><span>The only things that are purely </span><em><span>You </span></em><span>are the feelings you can attune to </span><em><span>right now</span></em><span> – in this moment, before you have the chance to filter them through your Constructed Self.</span></p><p><span>And maybe that’s why the present moment feels threatening to the Constructed Self. So it hides where it’s more comfortable – </span><em><span>almost there</span></em><span>.</span></p><h2><strong><span>So If There’s No “There”</span></strong></h2><p><span>“Almost there” is a trick. It lets you safely plan out exactly who you are going to be tomorrow, which means you don’t actually have to change a single thing today.</span></p><p><span>Your constructed self gets to stay perfectly safe, perfectly still, and completely untouched by reality.</span></p><p><span>But if there really isn’t any “there” to arrive at... then what’s left?</span></p><p><span>Just the thing that has always been right in front of us: here. </span><em><span>Doing</span></em><span>, not </span><em><span>becoming</span></em><span>.</span></p><p><span>Rick Rubin writes in </span><em><span>The Creative Act</span></em><span>: “We’re not playing to win, we’re playing to play.”</span></p><p><span>‘Almost there’ is an unfortunate human condition because it tricks us into waiting for a version of us that only exists in the state of waiting.</span></p><p><span>Waiting for real life to start.</span></p><p><span>But doing </span><em><span>is</span></em><span> life.</span></p><p><span>Like the whole thing… not arriving or achieving the ideal future self you built from an external blueprint. Just actually being here and doing the thing.</span></p><p><span>Because that’s the only place we’ve ever existed anyway.</span></p><p><span>Anyways :/</span></p><p><em><span>Thanks for reading</span></em></p>
