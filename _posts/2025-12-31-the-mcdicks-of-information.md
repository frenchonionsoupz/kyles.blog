school), you can relate:</p><p>Eat McDicks for the first time in a long time, and your body revolts.</p><p>Your digestive system simply isn’t prepared for the garbage you’ve subjected it to, and so you’ll pay the consequence in nausea or bodily expulsion.</p><p>But eat it again a few days later… and a few days after that… and that nauseous feeling goes away.</p><p>Your body becomes one with the garbaggio.</p><p>And it seems to me that the same sort of thing happens with scrolling on social media. At least, I notice the pattern in myself nearly every weekend.</p><p>That’s because I can’t normally keep any social media apps on my phone.</p><p>I’m a stupid little ape, and my limited willpower is a useless defense against the almighty algorithm.</p><p>I think about something Tim Ferriss said often:</p><div class="pullquote"><p>“All these companies are very smart. They have very good data scientists. They have very good UI specialists ... You’re bringing a knife to a gunfight if you think you can use your self-control to keep your use of Instagram to, say, 10 minutes.”</p></div><p>So I have a hard rule against keeping any social media apps on my phone. But I do redownload Instagram most weekends.</p><p>I use it to see if there’s any live music or comedy worth checking out. And also to stay connected with the kind folks I meet when I go out to shows or whatever.</p><p>And when I redownload Instagram, I’ll inevitably get sucked back in by the algorithm. I know there will be at least one point in the weekend where I waste 30 minutes of my life (<em>generous estimate</em>) scrolling through bullshit.</p><p>And the post-scroll comedown is similar to my first McDicks meal in a while:</p><p>A bodily disgust.<br>A “what the fuck did I just do with my life?” type of feeling.</p><p>Sometimes, it’ll leave me feeling angry or just defeated about the state of the world. Mostly, it leaves me feeling absolutely nothing.</p><p>But like McDonald’s, this post-scroll feeling goes away after just a few ‘scroll seshes’.</p><p>It just becomes normal – passing big chunks of time by stuffing my mind with empty calories – full for a minute, but nutritionally bankrupt.</p><p>My body becomes one with the garbaggio.</p>
