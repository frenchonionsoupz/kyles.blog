<p>“I don’t talk to that many people,” Rick Rubin begins.</p><p>“If multiple people are telling me, ‘<em>You’ve got to see this</em>’, chances are something in the universe wants this to happen, so I want to follow through. I want to do my part.”</p><p>One of those things that resonated with me instantly upon hearing it. Not necessarily because I’m into all that woo-woo shit… and I can be a bit turned off by the <em>it’s the universe maannn</em> vibe at times, just like everyone else…</p><p>But it sounded fun to me – a ‘rule of threes.’ If something crosses my radar three times, from three different places, then I should act as though it’s the universe giving me a recc.</p><p>Moving on.</p><p>I first heard of IFS (Internal Family Systems) through the Tim Ferriss Show. On a solo hike a few months after my Dad passed, I listened to Tim’s with the IFS founder, Richard Schwartz. That was one.</p><p>Soon after, I was listening to an Old Testament ep of <em>Matt &amp; Shane’s Secret Podcast</em>, when McCusker mentioned IFS. Stood out to me since this was such a random place to hear about IFS, especially since I had dug four years into the archives. That was number two.</p><p>My fiancé and I went into the hot tub of our apartment building one Saturday soon after. In our one year of living at that building, hitting the tub ~ once per week, this occasion was probably the only time we really met another group of people down there hanging out –</p><p>One guy, a friend of a friend of someone living there, was a therapist. And, if you haven’t already guessed it, he started telling us about how he’d gotten interested in IFS and was beginning to use it in his practice.</p><p>That was three.</p><p>This was right around the time I heard Rubin talk about the rule of threes idea, so it was top of mind. And I took it as a hint – look for therapists who use IFS.</p><p>It’s not that the universe is necessarily sending me a sign… I’m <em>acting as if </em>the universe was sending me some sign. </p><p>And in that moment, beaten down by the unexpected death of my father, feeling like I was thrown into a meaningless vacuum of time and space, I was very open to receiving some sort of sign.</p><p>SO I went looking for an in-person therapist in Denver who practiced IFS.</p><p>Austen was on one of the first few pages of whatever directory I used. We set up a conversation through Google Meet to see how we’d mesh.</p><p>“Since this is your first time doing therapy and this is your first conversation with a therapist, I’d honestly recommend having one or two more of these conversations with other therapists before deciding which route you want to go…” he said before we ended.</p><p>I ignored that advice. And my year and a half in therapy with Austen ended up being one of the most transformative of my life. </p><p>And while we only explicitly used the Internal Family Systems stuff a few times, I really do credit this “rule of threes” thing for being the reason why we met.</p><p>(i know i said before that … “it’s not that the universe is sending me signs, it’s that I’m acting as if” … but i think i’m just sort of hedging myself there so i don’t sound too crazy, but like… i think i really do believe i was supposed to meet Austen…)</p><p>Because whether or not the universe is actually conspiring to put certain things in my path, I get to decide what I do with them. And treating those things as invitations seems like a much more interesting way to live.</p><p>I call it – The Acoustic Algorithm.</p><h3><strong>The Acoustic Algorithm</strong></h3><p>I’ve given a lot of thought to ‘the algorithm’ and algorithm-driven feeds… I think because I have this very innate drive for independence/freedom, and I feel as though they are especially insidious in directing other people’s lives in ways that those people don’t even realize and are harmful to the individual’s quality of life (<a href="https://substack.com/home/post/p-156027513?utm_campaign=post&amp;utm_medium=web"><span data-color="#0000ff" style="color: rgb(0, 0, 255);">more on this</span></a>).</p><p>But like… it’s not like I want to be 100% free from external influence. That seems to only be possible for monks (...and maybe Tarzan). I just don’t want to have my attention and desires directed by systems designed to serve someone else’s interests.</p><p>But I <em>do </em>want to be influenced by other people. I want to stumble into things I never would’ve thought to seek out myself. I want the world around me to play some part in determining where my life goes.</p><p>So yeah… I introduce to you… The Acoustic Algorithm (brought to you by <a href="https://www.gibson.com/collections/gibson-acoustic-guitars">Gibson</a>).</p><p>An alternative to digital algorithms, where the people, places, ideas, and experiences you encounter in everyday life help guide your attention, interests, and decisions.</p><p>To me, it feels like a more <em>alive</em> way of approaching life… as though everything is an invitation from the universe to grow and learn in some way.</p><p>A movie that keeps getting recommended to you from completely unrelated directions. A place you’d never thought about that suddenly keeps popping up. The same strange idea surfacing in three different corners of your life.</p><p>And then you can sort of make up your own rules for this stuff. One I heard from Liz Gilbert that I loved:</p><p style="text-align: center;">“I love to get up really early at dawn on New Year’s Day and I go for a walk in the woods And the first animal that I see, the first wild animal that I see is my totem for the year, is my spirit animal for the year.</p><p style="text-align: center;">And then I go home and look up what that animal means. And then I just try to keep that with me for the year.”</p><p>My fiancé and I are gonna go to Romania next fall ‘cus we met some folks from there this past summer. I don’t know jack shit about Romania… and I know that’s not exactly the top European tourist destination for most Americans… but I don’t give a shit.</p><p>I’m treating that as an invitation – and I’m sure my trip to Romania is going to provoke something in me that’s valuable… some sort of curiosity, or emotion, or even just a thought.</p><p>That’s the sort of stuff that I mean and want to do more of… and it feels stupid to a degree, ‘cus it really feels like I’m saying “The Acoustic Algorithm” in this half-joking way to describe what boils down to simply participating in the world around us. </p><p>Like I’m <em>re-learning</em> how to live in this very basic way.</p><p>Welp.</p>
