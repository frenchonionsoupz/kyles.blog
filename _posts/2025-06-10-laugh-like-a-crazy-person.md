<p>My Dad had a loud laugh.</p><p>Guttural is the word that comes to mind. Infectious would be another.</p><p>On business calls. At dinner. When we watched Scrubs together.</p><p>He described his childhood as boring, and his old family photos say the same. My Dad and his brother look dreadfully bored in nearly every one.</p><p>Except for those photos that are most staged. Where the family put on appearances, if only for a few seconds.</p><p>Plus, my grandmother would tell Dad “only boring people get bored” which added some weird layer of guilt to the mix.</p><p>Anyway.</p><p>I like to think he always had that big laugh, even as a kid. </p><p>And maybe, through a childhood without much solace – bullied at school, ignored at home – he found ways to savor the rare good moments.</p><p>That when joy showed up, however briefly, he threw himself into it. </p><p>Even if just for a few seconds, maybe he could access a sort of presence that his body had naturally closed him off from for most of his life.</p><p>And so he laughed hard.<br>He laughed loudly.<br>He laughed fully.</p><p>I think it may be the greatest gift he passed on to me and my sisters. At least top 5…</p><p>And I want to extend the sentiment.</p><p>I’m lucky not to have any present trauma that closes me off from living fully in every aspect of my life. At least none that I know of.</p><p>The only thing that closes me off is myself – a sort of fear of putting myself “on the hook,” and so I default to living a life that’s not necessarily true to me:</p><p>I choose security over the things I feel most called to do.<br>I choose silence over the uncomfortable words I know are in my heart.<br>I choose unhappiness over uncertainty. <br>I choose knowing what to expect over growing into someone I don’t know at all.</p><p>On the Tim Ferriss Show, David Whyte explained:</p><p>“All of us spend so much time trying to find a path where we won’t have our hearts broken. And really, the only way you can find a path where your heart won’t break is by not caring. Finding a path where you don’t care about things or other people, that’s the ultimate protection against heartbreak.”</p><p>And I think it’s my innate tendency to drift onto that path.</p><p>So I have to check myself.</p><p>That’s why I’ve made it clear to myself, since my father’s passing, that my main goal in life is to “live fully.” That’s partly why I’m writing this right now.</p><p>This feeling started right after his passing.</p><p>I knew deep inside of me that the only thing I needed to do at that moment was fully feel what I was feeling. To grieve in the way my body needed to, without judging or avoiding any feelings.</p><p>And now, I’m hoping to carry that same sentiment into every aspect of my life.</p><p>To fully feel the feelings that arise.<br>To fully enjoy the moments with loved ones.<br>To fully recognize the finality of every moment, and live accordingly.</p><p>All this to say, I’m starting a new line of those tacky throw pillows. It’ll say:</p><p>“Laugh hard. Cry hard. Fart hard.”</p><p>Six words to sum up my goal in this life. </p>
