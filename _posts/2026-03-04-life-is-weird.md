<p>We think of “weird” as the exception, but I think it may be life’s default state.</p><h3>Nature is weird.</h3><p><strong>Slime mold</strong> has no brain, nervous system, or really anything we’d normally consider “alive.” Yet when scientists put it in a maze with food at the exit, it solves the maze.</p><p><strong>Cordyceps fungus</strong> turns ants into puppets. It hijacks the ant’s brain, steers it to the perfect climate, and bursts through its head to grow.</p><p><strong>Tardigrades</strong> are microscopic animals that are probably living in your backyard right now. And they also exist in outer space, volcanoes, glaciers… they just exist.</p><p>My point is this: when life is left to its own devices, it’s strange.</p><h3>People are weird.</h3><p>The thing I love most about music festivals (<em>specifically camping</em>) is how it allows people to be the freest and most uninhibited versions of themselves.</p><p>People stop performing and just live.</p><p>I happened to see some dude at Lost Lands who was casually strolling to the bathroom with a leash in his hand.</p><p><em>Oh that’s weird that there’s a dog at the festival.</em></p><p>My eyes peered down. Nope.</p><p>It was a loaf of French bread on a leash (yes, it had tiny little wheels so that it wasn’t just dragging on the floor… he wasn’t a barbarian). Fucking weird.</p><p>I see this weirdness with Deadheads, too.</p><p>I mean, let’s look at the typical Deadhead during the peak of their touring: quit their job, sold their belongings, moved into a van, and followed their favorite band on tour.</p><p>Fucking weird.</p><p>And of course people will point to “the psychedelics made them weird!” or even “the music made them weird!” I have a different perspective.</p><p>Maybe these things simply helped them access a part of themselves that is simply <em>more human</em>. And by “more human”, I mean: less controlled by the external “shoulds” that govern most of our lives.</p><p>That aliveness is what’s weird.</p><h3>What I take from this.</h3><p>I don’t think weird is a good or bad thing, really.</p><p>I personally tend to admire people who may be considered “weird” by most societal standards. But I don’t think it’s their weirdness I admire – more their ability to live in a way that’s unconstricted.</p><p>Not some “be weird” bumper sticker instructions or some forced personality that’s trying too hard to portray a certain image to others. Just… less friction.</p><p>The goal isn’t really to be weird, at least for me. It’s to be alive.</p><p>Aliveness just leads to weird.</p>
